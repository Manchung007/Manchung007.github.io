About

Kobe Man-Chung Tang is an Associate Professor at the Institute for Materials Research, Shenzhen International Graduate School, Tsinghua University. His research integrates molecular design, excited-state photophysics, nanoscale structural characterization, and device engineering to develop efficient and operationally robust organic optoelectronic technologies.

The group focuses on molecular vibration and isotope engineering for stable organic light-emitting diodes, advanced luminescent materials including phosphorescent and thermally activated delayed fluorescence systems, and the use of deuterium-enabled neutron scattering to resolve nanoscale morphology in organic photovoltaics.

Research

Isotopic Engineering and Vibration Control

Selective deuteration is used to regulate high-frequency molecular vibrations, reduce non-radiative losses, and retard degradation pathways in organic semiconductors under optical and electrical excitation.

Advanced OLED Materials and Photophysics

We design and study phosphorescent, TADF, multi-resonance TADF, exciplex, and circularly polarized emitters, with emphasis on deep-blue efficiency, color purity, and operational stability.

Organic Photovoltaics and Neutron Scattering

Deuterium labeling creates neutron-scattering contrast for non-destructive studies of donor–acceptor intermixing, phase separation, vertical composition profiles, and morphology evolution in bulk-heterojunction solar cells.

Device Physics and Degradation

We investigate the interplay among excitons, polarons, interfaces, molecular packing, and thermal or electrical stress to establish molecular and device-level design rules for reliable optoelectronics.
